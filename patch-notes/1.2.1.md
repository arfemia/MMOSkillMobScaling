Two things made scaled mobs read wrong, at opposite ends. A mob could stop taking damage from a weapon entirely, and the far zones never got any harder. Both are fixed, along with what made them hard to diagnose. Underneath, every scaled mob's numbers are re-derived around a player the mobs can hurt, so every server's mob numbers change with this version. Requires Ziggfreed's CommonLib 2.2.0 and MMO Skill Tree 1.7.0, which ship alongside it.

### The big fixes

- **Fixed: a scaled mob could be completely immune to a weapon instead of merely tough.** One player killed it in the usual number of hits. Another swung forever and the health bar never moved.
  - The server applies damage as a whole number, so a hit reduced far enough arrives as zero and still counts as a hit. A tanky mob, its armor and a resistance affix together could put an ordinary weapon under that line for good.
  - This mod's own reduction no longer takes a hit that was landing down to nothing. The mob's armor and resistances still apply afterwards, exactly as before.
- **Fixed: the far zones stayed low level.** Distance escalation started 15,000 blocks from spawn, further than players travel, so it never added anything. A Zone 4 spawn sat at whatever its zone mapping said, far enough under the Legendary band that the tier could not appear there.
  - It starts at 5,000 blocks now, the same radius as the protected ring around spawn. Leave that ring and group scaling and the distance ramp both begin.
  - If you already set your own value, nothing changes for you.
- **New: the mob inspector shows what the scaling did.** A tough mob and a mob nothing can hurt used to look identical on screen.
  - The crosshair panel carries the mob's health, the share of a hit it takes and the share it deals, each as a plain percentage, next to the difficulty it rolled at.

### The difficulty curve

- **Mobs are less spongy and hit harder, on purpose.** A plain mob at difficulty N is tuned as an even fight for a character whose power is N.
  - Health comes down hard. The old curve made a top-difficulty mob about two and a half times tougher to kill than an even fight wants.
  - Damage goes up a great deal. Mob damage grew far too slowly with difficulty to matter, and its ceiling was reached long before the difficulty ceiling was, so at the top of the scale a well-defended character could stand still and take almost nothing, whatever the difficulty said. A character's defense grows about five times faster than its damage over a full progression, so a fair mob at the top has to hit much harder without taking forever to kill.
  - The extra damage a point of difficulty adds is small at the low end and grows toward the top. A new character meets a mob that hits only a little harder than a plain one, while a top-level character meets one that hits as hard as the numbers above say.
  - Most of a mob's toughness now sits on its health bar, where you can read it, with a little left as quiet damage reduction. The two always multiply back to the same time to kill.
  - The health and damage ceilings sit well above what an even fight needs (a Boss at the very top of the scale comes in just under both). They stop runaway numbers without flattening the rarity ladder.
  - Casual, Hardcore and Playtest are retuned to three quarters, 1.4 times and 1.5 times the new curve.
  - Tuning by hand: `OutDamagePerPoint` is now `OutDamageScale` with `OutDamageShape`. Leave the shape at 1.0 and the scale is exactly the old per-point slope; the shipped shape is 1.4.
  - This lands with the lower defensive ceilings in MMO Skill Tree 1.7.0 and needs that version. They are two halves of one change, and either alone will feel wrong.
- **A Legendary is a Legendary all the way up.** A Legendary at difficulty 30 has the health and damage of a plain mob at 72, and it keeps its lead over an Epic at every difficulty.
  - A rarity used to be a bundle of health and damage multipliers on top of the curve. At higher difficulties they all hit the same ceiling, so a Rare and a Legendary became the same tank with different names. A rarity is now one number: how much further along the curve the mob is read.
  - Rare, Epic, Legendary and Boss are about a third more, three quarters more, well over twice and three times as dangerous as a plain mob at the same spot, and the gap widens a little further out. The Horrific spider overlay adds a quarter on top of whichever tier it lands on.
  - If you author your own rarities, the `Hp`, `OutDamage` and `InDamage` multipliers are gone: set the tier's `DifficultyMultiplier` instead. A file that still carries the old keys keeps loading, and the start-up log flags each one as an unused key, naming the file.
- **The top of the difficulty scale is about 20,000 blocks from spawn, down from about 101,000.** Distance escalation adds a point every 87 blocks (it was every 500), so walking out past the last shipped zone reaches the difficulties the scale goes up to. Owner overrides are untouched.
- **The word on the zone card means the same thing at every level.** It comes from how much harder a mob here hits than one that matches you, read off the same curve the mobs use, so the card and the mob can never disagree.
  - It used to come from the plain gap between the zone's difficulty and your power, and fifteen points is a very different fight at power 10 than at power 100.
  - Fair means you would last within a quarter of a matched fight either way. Hard and Easy run out to two and a half times faster or slower; Deadly and Trivial are past that.
  - Some zones change label, mostly the ones far above or below your own power. The difficulty number on the card is unchanged.

### Settings that are gone

- **The `Intensity` setting is gone, and a file that still sets it is named in the log, not rewritten.** It scaled every part of the curve at once, which hid the real numbers, and the health side it scaled has been replaced by numbers it cannot convert into. `/mobscaling intensity` goes with it.
  - At start-up, one log line per file names each file that still sets `Intensity`: your settings file, a world file of yours, or a settings file inside a pack (a world file inside a pack is only looked at while the mod is switched on).
  - The line gives the value it found, the curve keys that replaced what it scaled and what the file reads for them now, and a suggested starting point for the damage side only, marked as a suggestion. Curve keys that no longer exist are named the same way, with their values.
  - It repeats at every start until you edit the file.
- **Four settings that did nothing are gone.** `PresetMode`, and `AllowDifficultyIncreaseOnPartyJoin`, `LateArrivalBumpFactor` and `CompositionEnabled` under `OpenWorld`, were read in and never acted on.
  - They are removed from the settings, the admin panel and the reference file. A config that still contains them keeps working; the keys are ignored.

### More fixes

- **Fixed: a world with group scaling switched off no longer reports zero power to a boss fight.** It reports nothing, so the fight keeps its own posture.
  - This covers a world whose `AggregationMode` is `DISABLED`, before anyone has set foot in it as well as after. The `mmomobscaling:region_power` reading other mods' content can use behaves the same way.
- **Fixed: a world's own group-power setting is used.** A world that asked for the strongest nearby player (or the weighted fold) to set its difficulty was folded as the average, because every world read a number already combined under the global setting.
  - Each world's regions fold under its own choice now, and a change applies without anyone having to move.
- **Fixed: the Armored affix does something against most melee.** It names crushing and cutting blows too now.
  - Resistance matches the exact kind of damage a hit deals, and a family name does not cover its members, so naming only the general kinds did nothing against the swings that arrive as crushing or cutting blows. The six elemental wards were already correct.
- **Fixed: a world or floor file you named with capital letters is the file the mod edits.** On a Linux server, saving a world from the admin panel whose file you had named `Arena.json` by hand wrote a second `arena.json` beside it, and removing the rule missed the original.
  - A file is found by its name ignoring case, on every read, save and delete. The same goes for a name the mod would spell differently, such as `Arena Big.json`: it is read and saved as that file, with no second `arena_big.json` beside it.
  - If a folder holds two files that name the same rule, the log names both.
- **Fixed: a world that uses no zone or biome name prefix keeps that choice after a save in the admin panel.** An empty prefix used to come back from the Worlds tab as "inherit" and quietly switch the world to the default names.
- **Fixed: an item, loot-table or stamped-item reward you add to a rarity's or variant's loot reaches the player who made the kill.** Each one used to pay nothing, on every kill.
  - Item drops, drop tables and commands in the same loot were never affected. The shipped rarities and variants use none of these rewards, so this matters only if you added your own.
- **You can set the difficulty ceiling above player power, and the mod no longer tells you not to.** Player power cannot climb past its own maximum, so raising the difficulty ceiling is the only way for zone difficulty and distance escalation to reach further. It was never capped.
  - The start-up warning used to say your difficulty ceiling and the MMO's player-power ceiling should match, which is the wrong advice if you want mobs that out-scale a fully geared group.
  - It appears only when the ceiling is set too low now, where it flattens every strong group onto one difficulty, and it points at the setting that limits how hard those mobs hit.
- **A missing companion no longer takes the whole mod down.** The damage filter alone is skipped, with a line in the log saying so, and the mobs keep their health, their loot and their abilities. Before, it took the mod with it, `/mobscaling purge` included.

### Admin and tools

- **New: the admin panel edits the floors and shows you the fight.** A Floors tab lists every zone and biome floor, shipped or your own, and edits one in place. Leave a field blank and the shipped value stands.
  - In the Worlds editor you can target a world by its gameplay config key and exclude worlds by pattern, which is how you reach an instance world whose name changes every time it is created.
  - The Global tab's preview says what a number does. Each sample row shows the skeleton's real health, its real hit once the mod has seen one, and how many times longer it takes to kill than an unscaled mob. An amber note marks a row a ceiling is holding down; raising a slope past a ceiling changes nothing there.
  - Under the samples sits a rarity ladder: what Plain, Rare, Epic, Legendary and Boss fold to at whatever difficulty you type. Below that, a read-only panel shows the player power range the MMO hands this mod, how your minimum and maximum sit against it, and where on the MMO side those numbers are edited.
- **New: retune a zone or biome floor from your own folder.** Drop a file named after a shipped mapping into `mods/MmoMobScaling/difficulty/`, with just the `Floor` you want in it, and that zone starts from your number. Delete the file and the shipped floor is back.
  - A `README.txt` in the folder shows the format, and the start-up log names the folder.
- **New: you can say where distance escalation is measured from.** Set `Origin` under `DistanceEscalation` (an X and a Z in blocks; a minus sign is fine) in your settings file or a world file, or use the two new fields on the admin panel's Global tab and Worlds editor.
  - Leave either axis blank and it keeps following the spawn point. The protected ring near spawn stays centred on the spawn point either way.
- **New: a world can place its own HUD overlays and size its own regions.** These keys were accepted before and quietly ignored. The admin panel's Worlds tab has a row for each.
  - A world file may set where the zone card and the mob inspector sit (corner and pixel offsets), whether the zone card names the zone and biome and which name keys it uses, how far the inspector reaches and whether it shows a portrait, and how large a group-power region is in that world.
- **`/mobscaling purge` runs from the server console.** A player sweeps the world they are standing in, as before; the console sweeps every loaded world and reports each one in the log. This is the command for taking the mod off a server, which is when having to log in first is most awkward.
- **The distance settings are in blocks, and the file now says how they are measured.** They always were in blocks. The reading comes from the centre of the chunk a mob spawned in, so it moves 32 blocks at a time. At a threshold of thousands of blocks that makes no difference; it only looks odd if you test a few steps at a time.
  - The settings file also explains that escalation adds to the zone difficulty rather than replacing it, and that `/mobscaling inspect` tells you the distance where you are standing, so you can pick the number without guessing.
