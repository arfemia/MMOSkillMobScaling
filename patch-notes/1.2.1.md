Two things were making scaled mobs read wrong at opposite ends: a mob could stop taking damage from a weapon entirely, and the far zones never got harder at all. Both are fixed, along with the reasons they were hard to diagnose, and the numbers underneath every scaled mob are re-derived around a player who can actually be hurt. Every server's mob numbers change with this version. Requires Ziggfreed's CommonLib 2.1.1 and MMO Skill Tree 1.6.3, which ship alongside it.

- **A Legendary is a Legendary all the way up.** A rarity used to be a bundle of health and damage multipliers on top of the difficulty curve, and at higher difficulties every one of them hit the same ceiling, so a Rare and a Legendary became the same tank with different names. A rarity is now one number: how much further along the difficulty curve the mob is read. A Legendary at difficulty 30 has the health and damage a plain mob has at 72, and it keeps that lead over an Epic at every difficulty instead of losing it near the top. Rare, Epic, Legendary and Boss are about a third more, three quarters more, well over twice and three times as dangerous as a plain mob at the same spot, and that gap widens a little further out rather than closing; the Horrific spider overlay adds a quarter on top of whichever tier it lands on. If you author your own rarities, the `Hp`, `OutDamage` and `InDamage` multipliers are gone: set the tier's `DifficultyMultiplier` instead. A file that still carries the old keys keeps loading, and the server's start-up log flags each one as an unused key, naming the file.
- **Mobs are less spongy and hit harder, on purpose.** The curve is tuned so that a plain mob at difficulty N is an even fight for a character whose power is N, and measured against how a character actually grows that means two corrections at once. The health side comes down hard: the old curve made a top-difficulty mob about two and a half times tougher to kill than an even fight wants. The damage side goes up a great deal: the rate at which a mob's damage grew with difficulty was far too shallow to matter, so at the top of the scale a well-defended character could stand still and take almost nothing, whatever the difficulty said, because the ceiling on mob damage was reached long before the difficulty ceiling was. A character's defence grows about five times faster than its damage over a full progression, so a fair mob at the top must hit much harder without taking forever to kill. The damage side is also a curve rather than a straight line: the extra damage a point of difficulty adds is small at the low end and grows toward the top, because that is how a character's own toughness grows, so a new character meets a mob that hits only a little harder than a plain one while a top-level character meets one that hits as hard as the numbers above say. A straight line set right at the top would have hit a new character nearly twice as hard as an even fight should. If you tune the curve by hand, `OutDamagePerPoint` is now `OutDamageScale` with `OutDamageShape`: leave the shape at 1.0 and the scale is exactly the old per-point slope; the shipped shape is 1.4. Most of a mob's toughness is now on its health bar, where you can read it, with a little left as quiet damage reduction, and the two always multiply back to the same time to kill. The ceilings on health and damage sit well above what an even fight needs (a Boss at the very top of the scale comes in just under both), so they stop runaway numbers without flattening the rarity ladder. Casual, Hardcore and Playtest are retuned as three quarters, 1.4 times and 1.5 times the new curve. This lands together with the lower defensive ceilings in MMO Skill Tree 1.6.3 and needs that version; the two are halves of one change, and applying either alone will feel wrong.
- **The top of the difficulty scale is about 20,000 blocks from spawn instead of 86,000.** Distance escalation adds a point every 87 blocks now (it was every 500), so a character who walks out past the last shipped zone actually meets the difficulties the scale goes up to. Owner overrides are untouched.
- **The `Intensity` setting is gone, and a file that still sets it is named in the log, not rewritten.** It scaled every part of the curve at once, which hid what the real numbers were, and the health side of the curve it scaled has been replaced by numbers it cannot be converted into. So nothing in your files is changed: at start-up, one log line per file names each file that still sets `Intensity` (your settings file, a world file of yours, or a world or settings file inside a pack), the value it found, the curve keys that replaced what it scaled and what the file currently reads for them, and a suggested starting point for the damage side only, marked as the suggestion it is. Curve keys that no longer exist are named the same way, with their values. The line repeats at every start until you edit the file. `/mobscaling intensity` is removed with it.
- **Fixed: a world with group scaling switched off no longer reports zero power to a boss fight.** A boss fight in a world whose `AggregationMode` is `DISABLED` was told the region's power was exactly zero; it is now told nothing, so the fight keeps its own posture, and that holds before anyone has set foot in the world as well as after. The same goes for the `mmomobscaling:region_power` reading other mods' content can use.
- **Fixed: a world or floor file you named with capital letters is the file the mod edits.** On a Linux server, saving a world from the admin panel whose file you had named `Arena.json` by hand wrote a second `arena.json` beside it and removing the rule missed the original. A file is now found by its name ignoring case, on every read, save and delete; if a folder somehow holds two files that name the same rule (say `Arena.json` beside `arena.json`), the one spelled exactly in lower case is used when it exists, otherwise the first of them in plain character order (which puts a capitalised spelling first), and the log names both. The same goes for a file whose name the mod would spell differently, such as `Arena Big.json`: it is read and saved as that file rather than as a second `arena_big.json` beside it.
- **Fixed: a world that deliberately uses no zone or biome name prefix keeps that choice after a save in the admin panel.** An empty prefix used to come back from the Worlds tab as "inherit" and quietly switch the world to the default names.
- **Fixed: a scaled mob could be completely immune to a weapon instead of merely tough.** The server applies damage as a whole number, so a hit reduced far enough arrives as zero, and the swing still counts as a hit that did nothing. A tanky mob plus its armor plus a resistance affix could put an ordinary weapon under that line permanently: one player killed the mob in the usual number of hits, another swung at it forever and the health bar never moved. This mod's own reduction can no longer take a hit that was landing down to nothing. The mob's armor and its resistances still apply afterwards exactly as they always have.
- **Fixed: the far zones stayed low level.** Distance escalation started 15,000 blocks from spawn, which is further than players travel, so it never actually added anything and a Zone 4 spawn sat at whatever its zone mapping said. Far enough under the Legendary band that the tier could not appear there. It starts at 5,000 blocks now, the same radius as the protected ring near spawn, so the moment you leave that ring both the group scaling and the distance ramp begin. If you have already set your own value, nothing changes for you.
- **Fixed: the Armored affix does something against most melee.** Resistance is matched on the exact kind of damage a hit deals, and a family name does not cover its members, so an affix that named only the general kinds did nothing against the swings that actually arrive as crushing or cutting blows. It names those too now. The six elemental wards were already correct.
- **You can set the difficulty ceiling above player power, and the mod no longer tells you not to.** A warning at start-up used to say your difficulty ceiling and the MMO's player-power ceiling should match, which is the wrong advice if you want mobs that out-scale a fully geared group: player power cannot climb past its own maximum, so raising the difficulty ceiling is the only way for zone difficulty and distance escalation to reach further. It was never actually capped. The warning now appears only when the ceiling is set too LOW, where it really does flatten every strong group onto one difficulty, and it points at the setting that limits how hard those mobs hit.
- **New: the mob inspector shows what the scaling did.** The crosshair panel carries the mob's health, the share of a hit it takes and the share it deals, each as a plain percentage, next to the difficulty it rolled at. A mob that is tough and a mob nothing can hurt used to look identical on screen.
- **The distance settings are in blocks, and the file now says how they are measured.** They always were, but the reading comes from the centre of the chunk a mob spawned in, so it moves 32 blocks at a time rather than one. That makes no difference at a threshold of thousands of blocks; it only looks odd if you are testing a few steps at a time. The settings file also explains that escalation adds to the zone difficulty rather than replacing it, and that `/mobscaling inspect` tells you the distance where you are standing so you can pick the number instead of guessing.
- **`/mobscaling purge` runs from the server console.** A player sweeps the world they are standing in, as before; the console sweeps every loaded world and reports each one in the log. This is the command for taking the mod off a server, which is the moment having to log in first is most awkward.
- **New: a world can place its own HUD overlays and size its own regions.** A world file may set where the zone card and the mob inspector sit (corner and pixel offsets), whether the zone card names the zone and biome and which name keys it uses, how far the inspector reaches and whether it shows a portrait, and how large a group-power region is in that world. The admin panel's Worlds tab has a row for each. These keys were accepted before and quietly ignored.
- **Fixed: a world's own group-power setting is used.** A world that asked for the strongest nearby player (or the weighted fold) to set its difficulty was folded the same way as the average, because the number every world read had already been combined under the global setting. Each world's own choice is what its regions fold under now, and changing it applies without anyone having to move.
- **New: retune a zone or biome floor from your own folder.** Drop a file named after a shipped mapping into `mods/MmoMobScaling/difficulty/`, with just the `Floor` you want in it, and that zone starts from your number; delete the file and the shipped floor is back. A `README.txt` in the folder shows the format, and the start-up log names the folder.
- **Four settings that did nothing are gone.** `PresetMode`, and `AllowDifficultyIncreaseOnPartyJoin`, `LateArrivalBumpFactor` and `CompositionEnabled` under `OpenWorld`, were read in and never acted on. They are removed from the settings, the admin panel and the reference file. A config that still contains them keeps working; the keys are simply ignored.
- **A missing companion no longer takes the whole mod down.** The damage filter is the one part that names classes in other jars, to order itself against them. If one is missing, that part alone is skipped with a line in the log saying so, and the mobs keep their health, their loot and their abilities. Before, it took the mod with it, including `/mobscaling purge`.
